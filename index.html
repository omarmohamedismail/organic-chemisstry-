Introduction

History of Organic Chemistry & Classification of Compounds

Berzelius — Classification by Origin

Berzelius classified compounds according to their origin into two groups:

🌿 Organic Compounds

Compounds produced inside living organism cells. Examples: Acetic acid (CH₃COOH) — from vinegar bacteria, Formic acid (HCOOH) — from crushing ants

🪨 Inorganic Compounds

Compounds derived from solid parts of Earth's crust. Named depending on their structure, not origin.

Vital Force Theory

Organic compounds can only be produced in living organism cells by the effect of biological vital forces — and it was believed impossible to produce them in laboratories.

⭐ Compounds were named depending on the origin of their sources (e.g. Acetic acid → Acetum = oxidation by vinegar bacteria; Formic acid → Formica = crushing of ants)

Wohler — Destruction of Vital Force Theory

Friedrich Wöhler destroyed the Vital Force Theory by producing an organic substance in the laboratory from inorganic substances:

Reaction 1 — Forming Ammonium Cyanate AgCNO + NH₄Cl → AgCl + NH₄CNO
(Silver Cyanate + Ammonium Chloride → Ammonium Cyanate)
Reaction 2 — Rearrangement to Urea NH₄CNO —Δ→ H₂N-CO-NH₂
(Ammonium Cyanate → Urea)
⭐ Urea (NH₂-CO-NH₂ = CH₄ON₂) is the FIRST organic compound produced in a laboratory.
⭐ Urea is a fundamental substance in all mammalian urine.

After Wöhler's discovery, compounds were renamed depending on their structure instead of origin: CH₃COOH = Ethanoic acid, HCOOH = Methanoic acid

Steps for Naming

  • Step 1 — Find main chain: Longest continuous carbon chain (the parent chain)
  • Step 2 — Numbering: Number from both sides, choose the side giving the LEAST sum of branch positions
  • Step 3 — Name branches: Use prefixes: di, tri, tetra for multiples of same branch
  • Step 4 — Alphabetical order: When two different branches at same sum, give least alphabetical branch the least number
  • Step 5 — Assemble: (position-branch)- ... -parent chain + suffix
⭐ Numbering Rule: When numbering gives equal sums from both sides, give the least alphabetical branch the least number

Alkanes

Saturated hydrocarbons — CₙH₂ₙ₊₂

General Properties

General formulaCₙH₂ₙ₊₂     Functional group: C—H (single bond)
  • All bonds are single bonds (saturated)
  • Low reactivity — no functional group for easy reactions
  • Boiling point increases with molecular mass
  • Insoluble in water, soluble in organic solvents

Preparation of Methane (CH₄)

Lab preparation: Heating Sodium Acetate + Soda Lime

Preparation Reaction CH₃COONa + NaOH —CaO/Δ→ CH₄ + Na₂CO₃
Sodium acetate + Soda lime → Methane + Sodium carbonate
⭐ Methane is collected by DOWNWARD DISPLACEMENT OF WATER because:
• CH₄ is slightly soluble in water
• CH₄ has lower density than water
• Collected methane is "wet methane"
• To get DRY methane: replace water bath with mercury bath

Chemical Reactions of Alkanes

1. Combustion (Complete):

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

2. Substitution Halogenation (UV light):

CH₄ + Cl₂ —UV→ CH₃Cl + HCl    (Chloromethane)
CH₄ + 4Cl₂ —UV→ CCl₄ + 4HCl    (Carbon tetrachloride)

3. Uses of Alkanes:

  • Fuel (natural gas, LPG) — Propane in red cylinder (cold countries), Butane in blue cylinder (hot countries)
  • Production of reducing agent (water gas H₂)
  • Production of divided carbon (carbon black)
  • Production of halogenated alkanes (CHCl₃ — used in anesthesia)
⭐ Chloroform (CHCl₃): used in anesthesia but NOT SAFE — requires adjusted dose per patient or will cause death. Now replaced by Halothane (CHBrCl–CF₃) which is safer.

Alkenes

Unsaturated hydrocarbons with double bond — CₙH₂ₙ

General Properties

General formulaCₙH₂ₙ     Functional group: C=C (double bond)
  • More reactive than alkanes (π bond available for reactions)
  • Undergo addition reactions (not substitution)
  • Decolorize bromine water (Br₂/CCl₄) — key identification test

Types of Alkenes

🔵 Symmetric Alkene

Alkene where C atoms of double bond have the same number of H atoms

CH₂=CH₂ (ethene)
CH₃CH=CHCH₃

🟡 Asymmetric Alkene

Alkene where C atoms of double bond have different numbers of H atoms

CH₃CH=CH₂
CH₃CH₂CH=CH₂

Chemical Reactions of Alkenes

1. Addition of Halogens (X₂):

CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → CH₂Br—CH₂Br
(Ethene + Bromine → 1,2-dibromoethane)

2. Addition of Hydrogen Halide (HX): HBr, HI, HF, HCl

⭐ MARKOVNIKOV'S RULE: When adding asymmetric reagent (HX, H₂O, H₂SO₄) to asymmetric alkene:
→ The POSITIVE part of reagent (H⁺) attaches to the Carbon of double bond that is RICHEST IN H atoms

3. Addition of Water (H₂O):

CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O —H₂SO₄→ CH₃CH₂OH (Ethanol)

4. Hydrogenation:

CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ —Ni/Pt/Δ→ CH₃-CH₃ (Ethane)

5. Test with Bromine Water:

⭐ Alkenes DECOLORIZE bromine water (Br₂/CCl₄) — color disappears from red/brown
Alkanes: remain red (no reaction with Br₂/CCl₄)

Alkynes

Unsaturated hydrocarbons with triple bond — CₙH₂ₙ₋₂

General Properties

General formulaCₙH₂ₙ₋₂     Functional group: C≡C (triple bond)
⭐ Reactivity Order: Alkynes > Alkenes > Alkanes
(More π bonds = more reactive)
  • Two π bonds available for addition reactions
  • Most reactive of the three hydrocarbon classes
  • Decolorize bromine water (like alkenes)

Preparation of Acetylene (Ethyne)

CaC₂ + 2H₂O → HC≡CH + Ca(OH)₂
Calcium carbide + Water → Acetylene + Calcium hydroxide

Acetylene is used in welding (oxy-acetylene torch).

Cycloalkanes & Benzene

Ring-structure hydrocarbons

Cycloalkanes

General formulaCₙH₂ₙ     Same as Alkenes — distinguish by structure!
  • Same general formula as alkenes (CₙH₂ₙ) but different structure
  • Ring structure — no double bonds
  • More stable than open-chain alkenes

Benzene — Aromatic Ring

Molecular formulaC₆H₆     Structural: hexagonal ring with alternating double bonds

Preparation of Cyclohexane from Hexane:

C₆H₁₄ —Pt/Δ Catalytic Reforming→ ⬡ + 4H₂
Hexane → Cyclohexane + hydrogen

Reactions of Benzene:

  • Hydrogenation: ⬡ + 3H₂ → Cyclohexane (Ni/Pt, Δ)
  • Halogenation (UV): C₆H₆ + 3Cl₂ → C₆H₆Cl₆ (Gamexane — Insecticide!)
  • Nitration: C₆H₆ + HNO₃ → C₆H₅NO₂ (Nitrobenzene — used in explosive TNT)
⭐ Gamexane (C₆H₆Cl₆) is used as an INSECTICIDE
⭐ Methane + 4Cl₂ (UV) → CCl₄ (Carbon tetrachloride)

Alcohols & Ethers

Hydroxy and ether functional groups

Alcohols — General

NameFormulaType
Methanol (wood alcohol)CH₃OHPrimary
Ethanol (grain alcohol)C₂H₅OHPrimary

2-Butanol (sec-butyl alcohol)CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃Secondary

Physical Properties of Alcohols

  • Boiling point higher than alkanes of similar mass (due to H-bonding)
  • Miscible with water (short chain); decreasing solubility with chain length
  • At temperatures above 65°C → completely soluble in water
  • Alcohols are NEUTRAL (pH = 7)
⭐ Alcohols are NEUTRAL (pH=7) — the H of OH can be released as H⁺ but with such LOW concentration it cannot change color of litmus paper

Chemical Properties of Alcohols

Reactions depend on 3 parts: R group (whole compound), Carbinol (C—OH), OH group

  • Acidity test: React with Na ✓ | React with NaOH ✗ (too weak)
  • Esterification: Alcohol + Acid → Ester + Water (H₂SO₄ catalyst)
  • Dehydration: With H₂SO₄ at 180°C → Alkene; at 140°C → Ether
  • Oxidation: Primary → Aldehyde → Carboxylic Acid; Secondary → Ketone
  • Reaction with HX: —OH replaced by halogen

Phenols

Aromatic hydroxy compounds — Carbolic Acid

Structure & Properties

Phenol formulaC₆H₅OH     Boiling Point: 182°C
⭐ Phenols are ACIDIC (pH < 7) — NOT neutral like alcohols!
The C=O has HIGH electronegativity, making the H easier to release as H⁺ with HIGH concentration → enough to change litmus paper to RED
Therefore phenol is called CARBOLIC ACID
  • Boiling point: 182°C (higher than alcohols of similar mass)
  • Sparingly miscible with water; at 65°C becomes completely soluble
  • Acidic solution (pH < 7) — changes litmus to red
  • React with Na ✓ | React with NaOH ✓ (unlike alcohols!)

Carbinol Group — Bond Polarity

In the carbinol group (C—O—H):

  • C side has HIGH electronegativity → Bond is STRONG and SHORT → Difficult to break
  • H side has LOW electronegativity → Bond is LONG and WEAK → Easy to break
  • So H can liberate as H⁺ with HIGH concentration
⭐ In ALCOHOLS: H⁺ released in LOW concentration → NOT enough to change litmus color → NEUTRAL
In PHENOLS: H⁺ released in HIGH concentration → Changes litmus to RED → ACIDIC

Reactions of Carboxylic Acids

  • With NaOH: RCOOH + NaOH → RCOONa + H₂O
  • With Na₂CO₃: 2RCOOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2RCOONa + CO₂ + H₂O

Esters

Fragrant compounds — RCOORʼ

Formation (Esterification)

General formulaCₙH₂ₙO₂   (same as carboxylic acids — isomers!)
RCOOH + R'OH —H₂SO₄/Δ→ RCOO-R' + H₂O
(Acid + Alcohol → Ester + Water)
⭐ Esters and carboxylic acids are ISOMERS — same formula CₙH₂ₙO₂ but different structures

Saponification (Hydrolysis)

RCOOR' + NaOH → RCOONa + R'OH
Ester + NaOH → Soap (sodium salt of acid) + Alcohol

This is how soap is made — saponification of natural fats and oils with NaOH.

Aspirin ★

Acetylsalicylic acid — a key application of organic chemistry

Properties & Uses of Aspirin

Chemical name & formulaAcetylsalicylic acid   C₉H₈O₄

Aspirin is used for:

  • Anti-inflammatory (ضد الالتهاب)
  • Analgesic / Pain killer (مسكن)
  • Anti-coagulant (مضاد تجلط الدم)
  • Anti-pyretic — reduces fever (خافض حرارة)
  • Anti-headache (مسكن صداع)

Aspirin in the Stomach — Acidic Hydrolysis

Hydrolysis Reaction in Stomach Aspirin (C₉H₈O₄) + H₂O —H⁺→ Salicylic acid + CH₃COOH
(Acetylsalicylic acid + water → Salicylic acid + Acetic acid)
⭐ PROBLEM: This reaction INCREASES acidity of stomach → May cause ULCERS or BLEEDING

SOLUTIONS:
1. Take aspirin AFTER MEALS
2. Crushing aspirin BEFORE swallowing
3. Using COATED aspirin — coated by layers of Al(OH)₃ which decreases the effect by forming Aluminum salts

Aspirin Preparation — Esterification

Salicylic acid + Acetic anhydride —H₂SO₄→ Aspirin + Acetic acid
C₇H₆O₃ + C₄H₆O₃ → C₉H₈O₄ + CH₃COOH

This is an esterification reaction — the key reaction type for making aspirin in the lab.
